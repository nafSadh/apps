2026 Midterms · Results

Democrats take both chambers as a midterm wave reaches deep into Trump country

Democrats flipped the House, gained seven Senate seats and picked up five governorships, winning statewide in Texas, Kansas, Iowa, Ohio, Georgia, and South Carolina. In 66 of 70 Senate and governor races with a comparable count, Democrats improved on the 2024 presidential margin.

03 · The SenateSeven seats gained, five in states Trump won by double digits

Starting from a 47–53 deficit on a Class 2 map concentrated in Republican states, Democrats held all 13 of their own seats and flipped North Carolina, Texas, Maine, Ohio, Kansas, South Carolina, and Iowa for a 54–44 majority with Alaska and Mississippi still counting. Republicans held Florida's special election by 2.9 points and Nebraska by 6.7 points against independent Dan Osborn.

04 · Governors & Split TicketsFive governorships flipped, and where voters split their ballots

Democrats defeated Republican incumbents in Texas (Greg Abbott) and Nevada (Joe Lombardo), won open Republican-held seats in Iowa, Georgia, and Ohio, and defended every called Democratic seat, for a 27–20 majority of governorships with 3 still uncalled. Across the 26 states with both Senate and governor races on Tuesday's ballot, voters split party winners in New Hampshire (18.3-point gap) and South Carolina (8.9-point gap).

07 · The ElectorateWho voted how, and why

Six in ten voters said Donald Trump was a factor in their House vote, and those voting to oppose him outnumbered those voting to support him nearly three to one (44% to 16%). Women backed Democrats by 19 points and voters under 30 by 48 points, while independents broke 58%–38% Democratic. Voters naming the economy first favored Democrats 52%–46%, reversing 2024.

08 · On the BallotAbortion protections pass in every test; voter ID passes in eight states

Voters approved constitutional abortion-rights measures in Virginia (62.4%) and Nevada (63.3%), rejected a repeal of Missouri's 2024 abortion amendment (44.5% Yes), and defeated an abortion-restriction measure in Idaho. On the same ballots, eight states passed citizenship or photo voter-ID rules, Missouri voters rejected the 2025 congressional map (38.2% Yes), and California's one-time billionaire tax trails at 44.4%.

09 · History & 2028Nine midterms since 1994, state trifectas, and the 270 map

At NBC's projected 42-seat Republican House loss, 2026 matches 2018 (-41) and trails 2010 (-63) and 1994 (-54). What sets 2026 apart is the Senate: losing at least seven seats puts 2026 alongside 1994 (-8) and 2014 (-9) among the largest upper-chamber midterm losses in three decades. In the states, NCSL's preliminary count gives Democrats new trifectas in Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin, and breaks Republican trifectas in Georgia, Iowa, Ohio and Texas.
