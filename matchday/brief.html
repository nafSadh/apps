Man City found guilty on majority of 115 charges

Manchester City have been found guilty of the majority of the Premier League's 115 financial charges, BBC Sport and Sky Sports both report; FourFourTwo puts the number at 114 of 115. Chief executive Ferran Soriano struck a defiant tone with fellow club executives, saying the fight could take years to resolve, and declined to answer questions from Sky News as he left the meeting. Sky Sports and BBC Sport separately note that an appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport is not straightforward, so a final resolution remains distant.

Reaction from City's side has been mixed. Former manager Roberto Mancini distanced himself from the case, telling reporters he is ‘not guilty’ despite the finding, while appearing to acknowledge holding what the Guardian describes as a ‘double contract’ during his time at the club. Chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak continues to maintain City's innocence, and former goalkeeper Joe Hart says he trusts that account. Nothing here is an official Premier League statement — it is all club and third-party reaction, reported by BBC Sport and the Guardian.

Elsewhere, only rumour-tier items touched the six clubs tracked here. BBC's gossip column has repeated across two separate Sundays that Bayern are interested in Barcelona's Dani Olmo, and separately reported that Trent Alexander-Arnold will stay at Real Madrid — neither claim corroborated by another outlet. FourFourTwo reports Liverpool captain Virgil van Dijk could leave as a free transfer, for Milan; Transfermarkt's live blog credits both Arsenal and Liverpool with interest in Kevin Schade. Treat all of it as reported, not settled.
