Man City Found Guilty on All Charges; Punishment Still Open

Manchester City have been found guilty of all charges in the Premier League's financial-rules case, an independent commission citing serious breaches including a disguised-funding arrangement and about £900m in inflated revenue, per Transfermarkt. This is settled fact, not rumour, and genuinely corroborated: Sky Sports, BBC Sport, the Guardian, FourFourTwo and Transfermarkt each ran separate coverage of the verdict, rather than one outlet's story being reprinted elsewhere.

What happens next is not settled. Sky Sports reports an appeal process with dates and deadlines still being worked out, and FourFourTwo cites the Independent Football Regulator's chair saying it is prepared to scrutinise City's ownership. Pep Guardiola publicly backed the owners; Jamie Carragher, per Sky Sports, wants City stripped of titles won during the breach years. On Erling Haaland, reports conflict: FourFourTwo cites a single report he has already agreed to join Barcelona, while BBC Sport says Norway teammates describe him as unaffected by the verdict.

Elsewhere, BBC's Wednesday gossip column reports Real Madrid are waiting for Virgil van Dijk to go free before pursuing him, and that Bayern Munich have offered Michael Olise a long-term contract while eyeing Barcelona's Jules Kounde — all single-source and unconfirmed elsewhere. In the Women's Champions League, Sky Sports and the Guardian both covered Arsenal's trip to Paris FC, with Sky reporting Paris pegging the Gunners back late.
