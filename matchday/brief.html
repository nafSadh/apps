Man City's guilty verdict, disputed; Liverpool rehires Ward

An independent tribunal has ruled on Manchester City's long-running 115-charges case, and Guardian columns treat it as a guilty verdict — one writer says the consequences for the Premier League “could be profound.” The club disputes that framing: Sky Sports first flagged it only as “reports” of a decision, and a separate Guardian piece notes City “deny wrongdoing and say process is ongoing.” Chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak has reaffirmed his belief the club will prove its innocence.

Liverpool moved on the football-operations side: the Guardian reports FSG has rehired Julian Ward as sporting director for a second stint, replacing Richard Hughes, who left for Al-Hilal, amid what the Guardian describes as upheaval in the club's hierarchy. Separately, BBC Sport says striker Alexander Isak has rejoined the club after picking up a minor injury on international duty with Sweden — a fitness update, not a transfer story.

Elsewhere, only rumour-tier moves surfaced for the group. BBC's gossip column repeated that Bayern Munich are interested in Barcelona's Dani Olmo, an item it also ran two days earlier; no other outlet corroborated it. The same roundup reported that Trent Alexander-Arnold will stay at Real Madrid, also unconfirmed elsewhere. Arsenal's only notable result was on the women's side, where Chelsea beat them 1-0 in the WSL, as reported separately by Sky and the Guardian, ending an 11-month unbeaten run. PSG had no reportable news today.
