## What "the peak" is (asked directly, answered here for the record)

"The peak" is this guide's own coinage, defined in Lesson 03 (`#peak`): the half-second when
gesture, glance and geometry converge and the frame is worth ten times its neighbours — tested by
"does the picture collapse if the moment moves a half-second either way?" It is a deliberate
re-derivation of Cartier-Bresson's actual definition (simultaneous recognition of an event's
significance *and* the form that expresses it), stripped of the "decisive moment" branding the
guide explicitly debunks at `street-guide.html:648`. It is a **house term** — there is no external
citation for it and there shouldn't be; the honest treatment is a glossary card saying exactly
that, with lineage ("after Cartier-Bresson's 1952 definition") and a link to the defining lesson.
Same treatment for the other house vocabulary: *thumbnail truth · fishing-not-hunting · stage-first
vs subject-first · spectator geometry · working distance · timing-agnostic · pressing on presence
vs meaning · the loaded frame · transformation levers · background slice*.

## Evidence on what viewers actually respond to (Sadh, 2026-07-31: "look into what people like
## etc. based on proven citable sources")

The guide used to ground its audience claims in Sadh's Reddit scores; those are cut. There *is* a
real empirical literature here, and four findings map onto the guide's spine. **All four are
UNVERIFIED LEADS — found by search, primary sources not opened.** Every scholarly host tested
(`dl.acm.org`, `people.csail.mit.edu`, `proceedings.neurips.cc`, `cambridge.org`,
`semanticscholar.org`, `eegilbert.org`, `news.gatech.edu`) answers **403 to CONNECT** through the
web sandbox's proxy — the same block CLAUDE.md documents for the image CDNs. WebSearch works,
WebFetch does not. **Do not write any of these onto the page from this file.** Open each primary
source in a local session, confirm the numbers, then cite.

| Lead | Would substantiate | Verify at |
|---|---|---|
| Bakhshi, Shamma & Gilbert, *Faces Engage Us: Photos with Faces Attract More Likes and Comments on Instagram*, CHI '14, pp. 965–974 | §02 "the person is the photo" — with a measured engagement effect instead of an assertion | `doi 10.1145/2556288.2557403`; author copy at eegilbert.org/papers/chi14.faces.bakhshi.pdf |
| Cerf, Harel, Einhäuser & Koch, *Predicting human gaze using low-level saliency combined with face detection*, NIPS 2007 | §02 and §05 — faces are fixated within the first couple of fixations, so a face in frame decides where the eye goes before composition does | proceedings.neurips.cc, 2007 |
| Torralba, *How many pixels make an image?*, **Visual Neuroscience** 26 (2009) 123–131 | §06 "thumbnail truth" — the 200-pixel test currently rests on nothing but assertion | Cambridge Core; author copy `howmanypixels.pdf` |
| Isola, Xiao, Torralba & Oliva, *What makes an image memorable?* (CVPR 2011) and *Understanding the Intrinsic Memorability of Images* (NIPS 2011) | §06, §02, and the guide's whole self-assessment premise | web.mit.edu/phillipi/www/publications/UnderstandingMemorability.pdf |

**Read the memorability work before leaning on it — search summaries say it cuts against us in
one place and for us in two.** As reported (again, unverified): images containing people with
visible faces are among the most memorable, and vistas and peaceful scenes among the least —
which supports the guide's street-over-landscape spine harder than anything currently on the page.
But aesthetic beauty is reportedly **weakly and even negatively** correlated with memorability,
which does *not* support the line this pass just deleted ("description earns polite approval;
transformation gets remembered"). Do not resurrect that sentence with this citation attached; if
anything the finding argues the opposite and the honest move is to say so. Third finding, the most
useful of the three for this reader: people's *predictions* of what will be memorable correlate
weakly and negatively with actual memorability — i.e. the photographer's intuition about which of
their own frames will land is measurably unreliable, which is an argument for the contact-sheet
review in §04 that costs nothing to make.

Not yet searched, worth a pass: composition/aesthetics datasets (AVA, Murray et al. CVPR 2012),
eye-tracking on gesture and biological motion, and anything empirical on colour salience for §07.

**Standing caution.** This literature measures *likes, fixations and recall on crowdsourced sets*,
not photographic merit. It belongs in the guide as evidence about attention and memory, clearly
labelled as such — never as proof that a photograph is good. A guide that cites engagement studies
as an aesthetic standard has swapped one unearned confidence for another.

## Mechanism — three tiers, one endnote block as ground truth

Constraints: single self-contained HTML, no tooling, vanilla JS, keyboard accessible, must work
on touch (hover doesn't exist), must degrade with JS off, house palette/type, and Sadh's taste:
quiet, no academic `[1]` clutter, no poetics.

**A. Endnote block — the substrate.** A `<dl>` of full provenance entries (id per entry:
`ref-fanho-bullet`, `ref-nussenzweig`…) appended inside the existing Sources lesson as
"Provenance" (functional title, per the titles ruling). Each entry: who/where/when, edition or
URL, and an as-of date for anything that drifts. This is the JS-off, print, and screen-reader
path — hover cards are enhancement over it, never the only home of the information.

**B. Cite markers + hover cards — for P and A rows.** Wrap the claim's key phrase:
`<a class="cref" href="#ref-x">…phrase…</a>` — dotted underline in `--dim`, no superscript
glyph, accent on hover/focus. On hover, focus, or tap: one shared popover card (reuse the
lightbox's positioning/dismiss idioms; Escape and outside-tap close; `aria-describedby`;
respects `prefers-reduced-motion`) showing the endnote's content. Card content lives in the
endnote `<dd>` and is cloned — written once, shown twice. With JS off the marker is just an
in-page link to the endnote, which is fine.

**C. Glossary cards — for house terms.** Same card component, different source: a
`<template id="gloss">` map of term → two-sentence definition + "defined in Lesson NN" link +
lineage line where one exists. Marked on **first use per lesson only** with `<dfn class="gterm">`
(dotted underline, same quiet style). Every card for an **O-type** claim says plainly *"This
guide's own reading"* — provenance by honesty, not manufactured authority.

Density rules: at most one marker per paragraph (pick the load-bearing claim); quotes always
get one; figure-caption readings get **no inline marker** — instead one standing line in the
footer/Provenance intro: "readings of individual frames are this guide's own unless a curator
is named." Keep the footer's existing "verified against named sources" line true by making it so.

## Verification protocol — the hard rule

**No remembered citations.** Every P/A entry gets verified against the actual source (fetched
page, book in hand, court record) before its card ships; repo precedent is explicit ("do NOT
trust remembered names" — REWORK-PLAN §B8, and two of three baked annotations were wrong before
mock-up). A claim that fails verification is softened or cut, not cited anyway. Note the web
sandbox blocks some hosts (`maciejdakowicz.com`, squarespace CDN, `tile.loc.gov` — see CLAUDE.md);
run the fetch pass where egress allows, or verify those few locally.

*Bystander* / his Masters of Photography interviews; the Capa line to *Slightly Out of Focus*;
KaoRi's letter is her April 2018 note (Japanese original — cite it, not a paraphrase of it).

## Corrections expected to fall out of verification

- **Nussenzweig** (`:1773`): the "art is not advertising or trade" holding is the trial court
  (N.Y. Sup. Ct. 2006, aff'd App. Div.); the Court of Appeals affirmed on *statute of
  limitations*. "The settled case" phrasing survives, but the card must cite the right court,
  and the prose may need one clause of honesty.
- **"No reasonable expectation of privacy"** (`:1773`): that phrase is Fourth Amendment
  vocabulary (Katz) imported into a tort context. Either cite the actual privacy-tort doctrine
  or reword to the plain claim (photography in public is lawful; publication as art is
  protected) and let Nussenzweig carry it.
- **Institution counts** (`:1579`): MoMA/Getty/Smithsonian numbers change; pin each to an
  as-of date in its card rather than chasing them.
- **Winogrand rolls** (`:1419`): counts vary by telling (2,500 undeveloped + ~6,500 unproofed
  is the usual SFMOMA/Rubinfien formulation) — verify and phrase to match the source.
- **Ethics ethnographic claim** (`:1786`): currently stated as fact with no source; either find
  one (Alam's essays are the nearest lead) or rewrite explicitly as the guide's position.
